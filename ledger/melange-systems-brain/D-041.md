## D-041 - Graph shows its four analyses at once; Chart Studio spends its chrome on the chart
Date: 2026-09-17
Status: ACTIVE
Decision: The console's two analysis surfaces optimise for different things and the trade is deliberate. Graph's Overview shows all four analyses at once (recipient types, candidates, company comparison, employee level) in one shared panel chrome, each panel owning its own row limit, so its visible-control count may RISE; every bar carries its share of the figure its panel belongs to. Chart Studio spends its chrome budget on the chart: at most two rows above the canvas, one entry point per decision, and the chart's top visible without scrolling at 1440x900. A control that duplicates another entry point is removed rather than restyled, and a helper sentence that repeats a control the reader is looking at becomes a tooltip, a chip or a panel footer.
Context: the UI clean-up pass (UI1) after S5, Paul's brief of 2026-09-17 and his pick "Graph B, Chart Studio A"; design record audits/mentat_failings_20260915/UI_CLEANUP_DESIGN_2026-09-17.md; patch UI1_graph_studio_cleanup.patch. The Board (Canvas mode as 1, 2 or 4 sheet tiles) is ruled IN by Paul but deferred to its own build with contract studio_api.json v2.
Rationale: measurement, not taste. Chart Studio's chart began 787px down a 900px screen because five chrome rows preceded it, and three separate controls chose the chart type; conciseness there buys the reader the chart. Graph's four analyses were one per tab with no shared denominator, so comparing them meant remembering numbers across tab switches; two more panels and their controls are cheaper than that. Control count is therefore a means, never the goal.
Reversal condition: a Graph Overview that cannot be read at 1440x900 even at a reduced default row limit (today four panels at Top N 10 measure 1328px and need scrolling), or a Chart Studio toolbar that has to grow past two rows to hold a control readers actually reach for.
