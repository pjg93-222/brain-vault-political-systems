## DE-029: Sampling a population whose property can be censused
Approach: A bounded live FEC re-fetch, first 600 requests and then 300, designed to estimate with a confidence interval how much money a truncation fault in stored employer text could have wrongly excluded under [[ledger/melange-systems-brain/D-043|D-043]], across 29,816 Banking rows worth $32,538,668.49.
Failure mode: the quantity was directly computable from stored data and needed no sampling at all. A stored text that excludes today and is a prefix of one of the row's company variants is exactly a truncation candidate, so a census over all 29,816 rows bounds the exposure exactly: 112 rows and $4,823.06, 0.0148%, with zero candidates on the corporate side. The sample would have returned a wider answer than the one already available, and its extrapolation would have been unsound anyway, since the draw was targeted at the classes of interest rather than random over the population.
Root cause: the design assumed the property was only observable by asking the source, when it was a property of the stored string and the matching rule together. Two rounds of effort went into sampling design (early-stop rules, strata, seeds, tie-breaks) before anyone asked whether the question was decidable offline.
Would reconsider if: the property depends on an external value not held locally. That is true of FEC-side restatement, where only the commission knows the current text, and it was not true of truncation. The sample survived in reduced form as a targeted confirmation of the six classes the census identified, at 47 requests rather than 600: 41 rows compared, 41 identical, zero verdict changes.
