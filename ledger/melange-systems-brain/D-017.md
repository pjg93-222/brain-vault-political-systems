## D-017 — GLASSEYE closes a backgrounded fan-out on its task-notification, not its launch acknowledgement
Date: 2026-08-27
Status: ACTIVE — shipped and verified live; `fanout.ps1` sha256 `137283d366d7f487a686748e6041a20e98821ebe897214e68b554dbc233c6cfd`
Decision: `Get-GlassboxFanoutRecord` classifies a dispatch from what the transcript OBSERVED (`toolUseResult.isAsync == true`, equivalently `status == "async_launched"`), never from a requested flag. An async dispatch closes only on a later task-notification whose `<tool-use-id>` matches and whose `<status>` is `completed`; a synchronous dispatch keeps the old `tool_result` close. A dispatch with no `tool_result` yet is UNCLASSIFIED and emits nothing at all — it counts toward `derived` and `open`, and waits. Elapsed time comes from the notification's `<usage><duration_ms>`, passed as `-DurationMs`.
Context: measured on run-20260827T2049415311942Z-70c7f4b9d6 — three backgrounded subagents emitted `agent.completed` 350 ms / 1,028 ms / 476 ms after their `agent.started`, with `duration_ms: -1` on every event, while `ListAgents` showed all three still running four minutes later. See [[ledger/melange-systems-brain/DE-013|DE-013]] for the mechanism.
Rationale for the pre-ack deferral specifically: emitting a start before classification is known produces the wrong KIND, and `Get-GlassboxEmittedFanout` collapses `agent.*` and `task.*` into shared flags, so a later correct `task.created` would be suppressed by the earlier wrong `agent.started`. An unclassified emission is unrecoverable; a deferred one is not, and the acknowledgement lands ~2.4 s after dispatch against a 12 s watcher cycle.
Verification: 12/12 fixture battery with a NEGATIVE CONTROL in which the original code fails AC-1 on the same fixture (`returned=True`, `open=1` vs the fix's `open=2`); PowerShell 5.1 parser clean; live end-to-end showing four backgrounded dispatches classified `bg=True` with real durations 1459159 / 1674432 / 955021 / 494073 ms matching their notifications exactly.
Known residual, accepted: the six pre-fix events already in that run's ledger cannot be corrected in place, because the collapsed idempotency flags suppress re-emission for a correlation id that already carries `agent.started`. Rewriting them is a separate, Paul-gated question.
Reversal condition: the task-notification shape changes upstream (tag names, `<tool-use-id>`, or the `<status>` vocabulary), which would silently stop closing async dispatches. That is a re-verify trigger, not a design reversal.
