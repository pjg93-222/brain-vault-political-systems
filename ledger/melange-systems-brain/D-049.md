## D-049 - Filing versions: identity decides; a reused transaction id is not a competing version; ambiguity keeps its dollars
Date: 2026-09-19
Status: ACTIVE - applied as `12a880dc9` on codex/mentat-crypto (transfer index VERSION 4, PAS2 parser "5"), on Paul's repeated instruction to finish the reconcile lane's open items after the item was flagged to him as his; evidence in audits/mentat_filing_versions_20260919. Paul has not read the ruling record yet: if he rules otherwise, this entry is reversed, not defended.
Decision: Inside a key group (committee | report type | transaction id) a row is what its counterparty, amount, date and type say it is. The same identity in a later filing is one transaction re-reported (one kept, the rest superseded: by verified header order where headers establish the family, otherwise by the highest file number, the FEC's own convention). The same identity repeated inside one filing is one itemization loaded again (counted once). Identities that share neither the amount nor the date, or that name two different valid counterparties, are different transactions reusing a label (counted, flagged tran_id_reused). Only same-counterparty rows in different filings that agree on the amount OR the date, with no header, are ambiguous; they keep their dollars - the latest filed version is counted under an unverified_version flag. allocate() never blacklists a committee: withheld and invalid rows enter its outflow denominator as an explicit unresolved share.
Context: The rule committed in `a63fabefd` (written by a Codex lane on 09-10, METHODOLOGY v5) withheld every row of any multi-row key group unless a filing header settled it, and blacklisted the source committee. Measured on the real files it blocked $152.5M of clean transfer edges across 119 committees, held $52.6M unresolved and removed $7.63M from the counted committee-to-candidate total, because filers reuse line labels as transaction ids (`SE.5`, `SB22.n`).
Rationale: [[ledger/melange-systems-brain/D-040|D-040]]'s principle - a row loses its amount only where evidence contradicts it - applied to filing versions. A key collision is not evidence of anything, and no filing header can fix one. After the change no identity is counted more often than any one filing showed it (six ledgers, 1,624,835 PAS2 keys), and the rebuilt index lands almost exactly on the 09-08 VERSION 2 counts.
Dials: AMBIGUOUS_VERSION_POLICY in mentat/tools/fec_transfer_graph.py and FEC_PAS2_AMBIGUOUS_VERSION_POLICY in mentat/R/fec_beneficiary.R, 'count_latest' (default) or 'withhold'; both are part of their cache keys. The choice is worth $65,000 of transfers and $117,349 of PAS2 across three cycles. After any change: rebuild the index with `python mentat/tools/fec_transfer_graph.py --directory <fec_beneficiary> --cycles 2022 2024 2026 --filing-header-directory mentat/state/cross-reference/filing-headers`; R refuses an older index as stale and a stale index zeroes every transfer-derived number behind a `warn`.
